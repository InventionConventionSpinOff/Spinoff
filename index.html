What is Spin Off?

A smart soccer ball that turns every kick into professional-grade data

Professional soccer analytics have always belonged to elite clubs with expensive equipment. Spin Off changes that. We embedded a microcontroller, six-axis IMU, and PDM microphone inside a regulation soccer ball. Every kick fires a Bluetooth data packet to the companion web app in under 100 milliseconds, where our AI coaching engine, AMIR, turns raw numbers into instant, actionable feedback.

No subscription. No dedicated hardware beyond the ball itself. Just kick, connect from any Chrome browser, and improve.

Real-Time Speed

Kick velocity measured up to 70 mph with accuracy verified against a professional radar gun. Every session builds a personal speed history.

Spin Analysis

Gyroscope data classifies topspin, backspin, sidespin, and mixed spin at up to 1660 Hz,letting you see exactly what your foot does at the moment of contact.

AI Coaching

AMIR, our own trained model, analyzes every session and delivers targeted technique feedback after every five kicks.

Strike Quality

The built-in PDM microphone grades each kick from 0 to 100 based on the acoustic signature of contact,clean strike vs. mishit vs. off-center.

Shot Mapping

Mark your position and target on an interactive field to track shot placement patterns over time and across sessions.

Works Everywhere

A single HTML file, no server. Runs in Chrome, Edge, or Opera. Connects to the ball over Web Bluetooth in seconds.

How We Built AMIR

Adaptive Mechanics Intelligence and Response

What is AMIR?

AMIR is our custom AI coaching engine. It runs as two complementary layers. A fast rule based system derived from real coach interviews fires on every single kick, catching immediate technique problems. A PyTorch neural network we trained from scratch runs every five kicks to surface trend-based patterns consistency drops, chronic high launch angles, spin rate degradation from fatigue.

Both layers generate specific, prescriptive feedback using language drawn directly from the coaches we interviewed. Not generic tips, the same words a coach would actually say.

Full Technical Breakdown: PyTorch training, coach interviews, and feedback rules ▼

PyTorch Training Process

We trained a 3-layer fully-connected neural network using PyTorch. Our dataset: approximately 500 labeled kick samples collected from three local youth soccer coaches and twelve student players across two weeks of field sessions. Each sample included speed (mph), spin rate (RPM), launch angle, strike quality score, impact force, and a coach-assigned quality label, excellent, good, or needs work.

Training ran for 80 epochs with the Adam optimizer at learning rate 0.001, cross-entropy loss, and an 80/20 train/validation split. Final validation accuracy: 87.4%. The trained weights were exported to a JSON array and embedded in the web app so all inference happens in the browser, no server, no API call, no latency.

Feedback Rule: Weak Shots (under 25 mph)

  • "Your plant foot should land 6–8 inches beside the ball, pointing at your target."
  • "Drive through the center of the ball with your laces, not your toe. Toe kicks lose power before it reaches the ball."
  • "Follow all the way through, your kicking leg should finish above hip height."
  • "Use a 5-step approach to build momentum before contact."

Feedback Rule: Low-Spin Curve Balls (under 100 RPM with curve classification)

  • "Strike the inside edge, not the center. Aim for the outside quarter of the ball's face."
  • "Angle your approach 30–45 degrees to the target line."
  • "Snap your ankle outward at contact. That snap is what transfers spin to the ball."
  • "Use the arch of your inside foot, it gives a larger spin surface than the laces."

Feedback Rule: High Launch Angle (above 35 degrees)

  • "Keep your body over the ball, leaning back at contact is the leading cause of high shots."
  • "Strike the center or upper half of the ball. The bottom half sends it skyward."
  • "Your follow-through should stay horizontal. If your leg swings upward, that is exactly where the ball went."

The firmware runs a six-stage state machine, from the moment the kick threshold is crossed to the JSON packet landing in the web app, in under 300 milliseconds.

Kick Detection — State Machine

The firmware uses a six-state machine (IDLE → DETECTING → KICK_DETECTED → SPIN_SAMPLING → ANALYZING → SENDING) with a two-tier acceleration gate to eliminate false positives from ball handling, drops, or bounces.

When filtered acceleration crosses 5G (KICK_ENTER_G), the firmware enters DETECTING and immediately begins integrating velocity. A kick is only confirmed after the 160 ms window if the peak reached 7G (KICK_CONFIRM_G) with at least 150°/s of concurrent gyroscope rotation,and the threshold crossing count stayed at 1. Catches and bounces produce multiple crossings; a clean kick produces exactly one.

// Two-tier gate: enter at 5G, confirm at 7G + 150 dps gyro bool strong = (capPeakG >= KICK_CONFIRM_G) && (capPeakG >= 9.0f || capPeakGyroMag >= KICK_GYRO_CONFIRM); bool single = (kickCrossCount <= KICK_MAX_CROSSINGS); bool settled = (magF < KICK_SETTLE_G); if (strong && single && settled) { enterState(S_KICK_DETECTED); } else { // Rejected — back to IDLE with 300 ms refractory period refractoryUntil = millis() + (REFRACTORY_MS / 2); enterState(S_IDLE); }

A 600 ms refractory period after each confirmed kick prevents a single strike from double-registering as the ball decelerates off the foot.

Adaptive Signal Processing

A fixed-alpha low-pass filter forces a trade-off: fast response loses noise suppression, slow response misses peaks. The firmware solves this by switching the filter coefficient based on the current state,fast during impact so no peak is clipped, slow at rest so gravity noise averages out completely.

// Alpha switches with state,fast during impact, slow at rest float currentAlpha() { switch (state) { case S_DETECTING: case S_KICK_DETECTED: return 0.85f; // ALPHA_IMPACT case S_SPIN_SAMPLING: return 0.50f; // ALPHA_SPIN default: return 0.35f; // ALPHA_IDLE } } // Applied every sample: smoothed = alpha * raw + (1 - alpha) * previous fAx = a*ax + b*fAx; fAy = a*ay + b*fAy; fAz = a*az + b*fAz;

The firmware also runs a live rest re-calibration: whenever the ball is stationary, the gravity vector and gyro bias estimates are nudged toward the current filtered readings at a rate of 3% per sample, keeping zero-point accuracy intact across temperature shifts and orientation changes.

Physics Engine — Velocity Integration

Previous firmware estimated speed from peak acceleration (the impulse approximation). The new firmware integrates acceleration directly over the impact window, physically accurate for any kick style, not just hard flat strikes.

To integrate accurately, gravity must be subtracted from every reading. But as the ball rotates, the gravity vector rotates too. The firmware tracks it in real time using the gyroscope via a cross-product rotation step each sample:

// Rotate the tracked gravity vector using gyro angular velocity float omX = fGx * 0.01745329f; // deg/s to rad/s float omY = fGy * 0.01745329f; float omZ = fGz * 0.01745329f; // Cross-product rotation step each IMU sample gravKx += (omY*gravKz - omZ*gravKy) * dt; gravKy += (omZ*gravKx - omX*gravKz) * dt; gravKz += (omX*gravKy - omY*gravKx) * dt; // Subtract rotated gravity, then integrate to velocity float linAx = (rawAx - gravKx) * 9.81f; velX += linAx * dt;

Launch angle is read directly from the final velocity vector atan2(|vZ|, vHoriz) rather than from flight time. This works even for ground passes and shots into the net that never leave the ground.

BLE Data Transmission

All kick data is packed into a single self-describing JSON object and streamed over one BLE characteristic in 160-byte chunks with a 20 ms gap between chunks. The web app reassembles them and parses the full object. This replaces the previous three-characteristic pipe-delimited format and adds session stats, personal bests, battery level, and coaching recommendations to every packet.

// Single JSON characteristic, complete kick payload { "kick": { "number": 12, "classification": "curve_ball", "powerScore": 74 }, "motion": { "speedMph": 38.4, "launchAngle": 22.0, "curlDegrees": 11.2 }, "spin": { "rateRPM": 212, "axis": "sidespin_left", "mixedSpin": false }, "strike": { "quality": 83, "classification": "clean", "contactMs": 9 }, "session": { "totalKicks": 12, "consistencyScore": 77, "improvementTrend": "improving" }, "personalBests": { "speedMph": 54.1, "newBestBroken": false }, "battery": { "percentage": 82, "lowBattery": false }, "recommendations": ["Focus on a repeatable foot contact point"] }

The single-characteristic design means one BLE notification subscription, no race condition between multiple characteristics, and easy extensibility, new fields are just added to the JSON object.

AMIR Architecture

AMIR runs as two independent layers that both contribute to the coaching output in the app.

Layer 1 — Rule Engine (fires every kick): Explicit thresholds derived from coach interviews. Speed under 25 mph triggers power tips. Spin under 100 RPM on a curve ball triggers spin mechanics tips. Launch angle above 35 degrees triggers body-position tips. Multiple rules can fire on the same kick with priority weighting.

Layer 2 — Neural Network (fires every 5 kicks): A 3-layer fully-connected network trained with PyTorch on 500 labeled kick samples (input: 5 features, hidden: 32 units each, output: 3 classes). Weights are a 4 KB JSON array embedded in the web app, all inference happens in the browser with no server or API call. Final validation accuracy: 87.4%.

On-device session analysis: The firmware itself tracks a rolling window of up to 80 kicks and computes a consistency score (coefficient of variation of speed) and improvement trend (early vs. late half power score average). Every 5 kicks it generates up to 3 targeted recommendations from a rule set grounded in the coach interviews, these travel inside the JSON packet so the app can surface them immediately.

Personal bests tracking: The firmware tracks peak speed, power score, spin RPM, and strike quality. When a new best is set, newBestBroken: true and whichBest are included in the payload so the app can trigger a celebration in real time.

Press & About

Project overview, validation data, and resources for judges and press

Project at a Glance

Spin Off is a professional-grade soccer ball analytics system built entirely by two 7th-grade students over five months. It measures kick speed, spin rate, launch angle, flight time, impact force, and strike quality using embedded sensors, transmits data via Bluetooth 5.0, and delivers AI-powered coaching through a self-contained web application.

The project was created for Invention Convention 2026 and validated through field testing.

The Problem

Professional soccer clubs use multi-camera tracking systems, GPS vests, and force-plate sensors to measure player performance. These systems cost tens of thousands of dollars and require dedicated technical staff to operate.

Youth players, school teams, and recreational players, who represent the vast majority of people who play the sport, have no access to this data. They improve by feel and guesswork. Or they don't improve at all.

Our Solution

A sensor assembly small enough to fit inside a regulation soccer ball, powered by a 1000 mAh LiPo, broadcasting to any Chrome browser over Bluetooth. Total additional hardware cost per ball is under $40.

The companion web app is a single HTML file with no server, no login, no subscription. AMIR, the AI coaching engine, runs entirely in the browser. Any player with a phone can get the same quality of performance analysis that was previously reserved for professionals.
